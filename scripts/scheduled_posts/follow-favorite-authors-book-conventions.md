Figuring out how to follow favorite LitRPG authors at book conventions is much easier when you stop juggling ten screenshot tabs from Discord and start using a convention app that links an author's panel schedule directly to their signing booth and book catalog. A unified author profile, for a signed Book 1 hardcover or a world-building Q&A, keeps you in the right room at the right hour.

At LitRPG Con 2026, about 600 app users used ConRoster—the convention platform built for gaming, fan and book conventions—logging 1,135 quest completions from 223 users and about 16,000 schedule shares. Here is how to track your favorite authors from their first pre-con announcement all the way to Sunday afternoon's last signing table.

## What is the smartest way for how to follow favorite LitRPG authors at book conventions?

The smartest way for how to follow favorite LitRPG authors at book conventions is pairing the author's early announcement with the convention's released schedule app, where their panels, signing hours, and dealer hall booth pin share a single live profile. That keeps outdated social screenshots from sending you to the wrong ballroom.

Authors often mention in a winter newsletter or Discord post that they are heading to a summer convention like LitRPG Con. That early heads-up tells you which series hardcovers to set aside on your shelf. What a January post cannot tell you is whether their Saturday afternoon panel will land in Ballroom A at 1:00 pm or Cedar Room at 3:30 pm.

Once the convention releases its official schedule in the app, treat that live schedule as your source of truth on the floor:
* **Date your screenshots:** If you save an early graphic from social media, replace it with your saved in-app schedule as soon as the program goes live.
* **Separate panels from signings:** A 50-minute group panel on progression pacing is for listening and Q&A; a listed signing slot or booth window is where you bring your stack of books.
* **Leave a travel buffer:** Give yourself twenty minutes between a panel on the second floor and a signing queue in the dealer hall so a slow elevator never stresses your day.

## How should you build a personal schedule that survives long signing queues?

Build your personal convention schedule around one top-priority signing and one anchor panel per day, using ConRoster's automatic conflict checker to flag overlapping sessions. Add a fallback browsing block or QR quest nearby so you always have a fun option if a signing queue wraps around the aisle.

Picture an invented reader, Della Finch, planning her Saturday morning. Della's main goal is getting her well-worn Book 1 paperback signed at 11:00 am, and she also wants to hear a 10:00 am panel on party-based dungeon crawlers.

When Della saves both sessions in ConRoster, she checks the walking route on the interactive map before 10:00 am even starts. She sees that the 10:00 am panel ends ten minutes before the 11:00 am signing opens, and she checks the booth storefront to see if she can place an item hold on the new Book 4 hardcover at the table. If a second panel at 11:30 am overlaps her signing window, ConRoster's conflict checker flags the clash immediately so Della can pick a 1:00 pm panel instead.

## What is the best way to talk with an author at a busy signing table?

Start by telling the author one specific scene, character choice, or system twist you loved in their book, and keep a sticky note with your name spelled out on the title page. A focused compliment sparks a genuine one-minute conversation without holding up the forty readers waiting behind you.

Authors spend months alone at a keyboard wondering whether a particular emotional payoff or boss fight landed with readers. Hearing "I loved how the blacksmith finally used her crafting flaw to break the siege in Book 2" lights up an author's entire afternoon far more than a generic question about when Book 7 is coming out.

Keep the table interaction smooth for everyone in the aisle:
* **Have your book out of your bag** before you reach the front of the line.
* **Match the setting:** Save deep spoiler debates for spoiler-marked panels and keep manuscript pitches out of the signing line.
* **Ask for a specific recommendation:** If you are new to an author's table, tell them what you normally enjoy ("I love slow-burn town building with low-stakes humor—which of your series should I grab first?") so they can hand you the exact right starter volume.

## How do you stay updated during the con—and follow authors after Sunday teardown?

During the convention, cache your schedule on lobby Wi-Fi so it works offline in basement rooms, and check your room-change inbox whenever you reconnect on the main floor. After the weekend, jot down why each discovered series caught your eye and follow authors through their verified public links.

If a popular author panel outgrows its room at 2:10 pm and moves across the hall, ConRoster sends the update to the room-change inbox, and volunteers post a sign at the old door for anyone whose phone was offline underground.

When you get back to your hotel room on Sunday night, spend five minutes logging the series you discovered while the conversations are fresh.
