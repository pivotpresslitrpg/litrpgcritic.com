The most useful check on an overpowered protagonist is not always a stronger opponent. Sometimes the objective is something his best attack cannot accomplish. *Apocalypse BREAKER 4* by Aaron Renfroe and Sean Oswald puts that problem near the centre through Cruz's damaged condition and Dean's search for help.

The hunting pressure is severe as well: dangerous quarantined combatants have been released against him. But the rescue work keeps combat supremacy from being the only measure of progress.

## Different objectives demand different tools

The dragon egg, the Scholar-Prime material, and the effort to help Cruz give Dean problems involving knowledge, protection, and competing interests. These are useful changes of tactical shape. An encounter can require keeping something alive, obtaining help, or understanding a bargain rather than clearing the room.

The remade warriors broaden the campaign again. Building an army changes the scale of Dean's responsibilities, but its members cannot be evaluated solely as disposable additions to a force total.

## The cost of the expansion

There are many moving parts: pursuit, recovery, quests, conspiracy, and increasingly substantial forces under Dean's influence. That variety can sustain a long progression-fantasy instalment, though it also makes the central objective easier to lose among the subplots.

The reader-fit question is straightforward. If the appeal is seeing an unusual build tested against different kinds of problems, this volume has a clear direction. If the appeal is a tightly bounded adventure with a small cast, the expanding campaign may feel diffuse.

The supplied edition also includes a canonical *Magic Eater* bonus story. That is additional material, not evidence that the main novel's final act belongs to a different protagonist.
