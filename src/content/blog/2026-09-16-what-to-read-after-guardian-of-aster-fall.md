[LitRPG](/blog/what-is-litrpg/) is a genre in which characters level up, gain skills, and navigate game-like systems as a core part of the narrative. It is characterized by visible stat progression, strategic combat, and a world whose rules the reader learns alongside the protagonist.

Guardian of Aster Fall hits a particular sweet spot: disciplined progression systems, characters who earn every power spike, and a sense that the world has genuine depth waiting to be uncovered. If that combination is what you're chasing, the picks below deliver it from different angles. You can also run your own searches on [LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) to filter by trope, sub-genre, or pacing style.

## What Books Are Most Similar to Guardian of Aster Fall?

The closest matches share at least two of these traits: an underdog protagonist clawing up a competitive hierarchy, a richly detailed power system, and world-building that rewards patient readers.

### 1. Fire and Song (Warformed: Stormweaver, Book 2) — Bryce O'Connor

Fire and Song picks up with Reidon Ward, who has climbed from the weakest cadet at the Galens Institute to one of the strongest in his class — and his Combat Armor Device, Shido, is now regarded as the most terrifying CAD anyone has seen. That underdog-to-rising-threat arc, set inside a competitive academy tournament structure, maps closely onto the kind of earned progression that makes Guardian of Aster Fall so satisfying. [Progression fantasy](/blog/what-is-progression-fantasy/) readers will feel right at home.

### 2. He Who Fights with Monsters 2 — Shirtaloon (Travis Deverell)

Jason Asano is settling into life as an interdimensional wizard adventurer when a city-wide contest pulls him into competition against the world's young elites. The blend of [portal fantasy](/blog/what-is-isekai/) and LitRPG, the party-building mechanics, and the slow reveal of a vast enemy threat give this entry the same layered quality fans of Guardian of Aster Fall tend to love.

### 3. Carl's Doomsday Scenario (Dungeon Crawler Carl, Book 2) — Matt Dinniman

A New York Times bestseller, this entry in the Dungeon Crawler Carl series drops Carl and Princess Donut into the real games after the training levels conclude. It's faster and more irreverent in tone, but the strategic leveling, the alien-constructed dungeon ecosystem, and the escalating stakes scratch the same itch. Browse the [books like Dungeon Crawler Carl](/lists/books-like-dungeon-crawler-carl/) list if you want to go deeper into that vein.

### 4. Domestication: Battle Mage Farmer, Book 1 — Seth Ring

Retired mage John Sutton wants a quiet farm on the outskirts of empire — and instead gets a world whose secrets have been buried under decades of war, plus a Doom Points mechanic that ticks toward apocalypse if he isn't careful. Seth Ring layers slice-of-life pacing over genuine strategic depth, and the world reveals itself slowly in a way that rewards readers who liked Guardian of Aster Fall's world-building patience.

### 5. Overpowered Wizard — Hunter Mythos

Exactly what it says on the tin, and that's not a criticism. This debut entry in the Overpowered Wizard series offers LitRPG with a protagonist whose magical power curve is the central spectacle. If Guardian of Aster Fall's power-fantasy elements were your primary draw, this is the most direct hit on that feeling.

### 6. Apocalypse BREAKER 2 — Aaron Renfroe & Sean Oswald

Two combat veterans sent back 130 years before the collapse of Harmonia — no stats, no skills, just brutal experience and foreknowledge. The [system apocalypse](/blog/what-is-system-apocalypse/) framing is tighter here, but the strategic-minded protagonists and the sense that every resource decision matters will feel familiar to Guardian of Aster Fall readers.

### 7. Restarting the Apocalypse (Book 5) — Michael Chatfield

Len and Rick lost the war once. Now they're back before the collapse with nothing but tactical knowledge and each other. This series rewards readers who want to see characters use hard-won expertise to reshape a doomed timeline — a kind of mastery fantasy that pairs well with Guardian of Aster Fall's emphasis on earned power.
