A LitRPG cover has one of the harder briefs in commercial art: signal fantasy, signal progression, signal the exact subgenre — dungeon core reads differently from cultivation, which reads differently from system apocalypse — and do it all at thumbnail size, next to forty competitors, in under a second. Artists who can do this are not decoration for the genre. They are load-bearing.

They are also chronically hard for authors to find, which means the reverse is true: authors are hard for artists to find. That's the gap worth talking about, because it just got structurally smaller.

## Where Do Fantasy Artists Find Book Cover Commissions?

The traditional answer is scattered: social media portfolio posts, general freelance marketplaces where your dungeon core cover sits next to logo design gigs, word of mouth inside author Discords you have to already know about.

[LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) now offers a direct route: a **Visual Artist application** into its vetted [Industry Professionals directory](https://litrpgtools.com/industry-professionals), where LitRPG and progression fantasy authors go specifically to hire. The visual artist path is deliberately broader than covers alone — it covers cover design, illustration, character art, maps, typography, and other visual work, because a series that sells commissions more than one image. Ebook covers, audiobook art, character pieces, world maps, hardback interiors, ad creative: one author relationship can become a production pipeline.

The audience is the point. Nobody browsing that directory is idly shopping. They're authors and publishers in your genre with a book in production and a budget.

## How the Visual Artist Application Works

The application is free, runs through a three-step wizard with auto-save, and gets human review before anything goes public. The portfolio requirements tell you what the platform is optimizing for:

1. **A hosted gallery of 5–10 images.** Originals stay private — the platform strips metadata and publishes only moderated display copies after approval.
2. **Honest AI disclosure on every image.** Each piece is classified as human-made, AI-enhanced, or AI-generated, with detail required for anything not fully human. More on why this matters below.
3. **A recorded rights attestation** that you created the work and control the rights you're licensing.
4. **Author verification.** You can have an author you've worked with personally vouch that the collaboration happened — a portfolio claim with a witness.
5. **Commercial pricing.** Every listed price must include a commercial publishing license. The platform pushes you to state openings and realistic turnaround, which protects you from your own optimism as much as it protects authors.

Approved artists get a dashboard with analytics — profile views, unique viewers, contact reveals, and the search terms authors used to find you — plus a pause button for when the queue is full. Authors and publishers on paid plans can also post **casting calls**: private paid briefs matched to approved artists whose style, availability, and rates fit. Your private rates and hard limits are never shown to the poster.

## Why "Human-Made" Is Now a Selling Point

Here's the editorial stance, and I'll make it bluntly: the flood of generated art has made *verified human artists more valuable, not less.* Readers in this community notice AI covers and talk about them. Authors know it, and the serious ones are actively looking for artists whose work they can stand behind.

That's why the disclosure regime is the most artist-friendly feature on the platform. When every image in the directory carries an honest classification and a recorded rights attestation, "this artist is the real thing" stops being a claim and becomes a checkable fact. The vetting isn't bureaucracy — it's the moat around your rates. A curated directory of disclosed, rights-clean, genre-fluent artists is the opposite of a race to the bottom.

This is also, frankly, what the community-building is for: putting the humans who write these books and the humans who illustrate them in the same room, with enough structure that both sides can trust the handshake.

## What Should Be in a LitRPG Portfolio?

If you're assembling your 5–10 images for the application, curate for the genre's actual demand:

- **Covers that work at thumbnail size** — this is the craft skill authors are really buying
- **Subgenre range** — a gritty system-apocalypse piece and a cozy crafting piece prove flexibility
- **Character art** with gear and progression cues; this genre loves a visible build
- **A map or interior piece** if you have one — series work is where the recurring income lives
- **Typography-forward work**, because LitRPG titling is its own visual dialect
