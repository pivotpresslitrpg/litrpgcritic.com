[LitRPG](/blog/what-is-litrpg/) is a fiction genre that fuses narrative storytelling with explicit RPG-style game mechanics — character sheets, skill progression, and leveled abilities embedded directly into the plot. It is characterized by transparent stat systems, measurable character growth, and a reader experience that rewards close attention to mechanical detail.

*Shadow in Madness* (Spite the Dark Book 2) by Aaron Renfroe is a confident, well-constructed second entry in a dark [isekai LitRPG](/blog/what-is-isekai/) series that earns its complexity. It opens with a character sheet that doubles as a worldbuilding document, and the pages that follow demonstrate Renfroe actually knows how to make those numbers matter inside a scene.

This is a review based on the opening material of Book 2. The series is ongoing.

---

## What Is Spite the Dark About?

*Spite the Dark* centers on Kaden Yamaguchi, a former special operations assassin now operating as a Hunter — a Catalyst-bonded operative navigating a world where an alien substance called SPITE has fused with his body, and creatures from a parallel dimension called the Madness are bleeding into Earth. Book 2, *Shadow in Madness*, picks up mid-mission: Kaden is surveilling a senator with connections to a shadowy faction called Gentex, while managing an AI companion named Samantha, an aging handler named Vasilis, and a summoned entity — Paisley, a Ziltrax locksmith — that looks like a winged millipede with a woman's torso emerging from its underside.

If that last sentence made you sit up straighter, you're the right reader for this book.

---

## How Does the Progression System Work in Shadow in Madness?

The progression system in *Shadow in Madness* is one of the more thoughtfully layered in recent dark LitRPG. Renfroe distinguishes between two parallel advancement tracks — Catalytic progression and SPITE progression — and then makes that distinction narratively meaningful rather than just mechanically decorative.

Kaden's Catalyst abilities govern his special powers: Summoning, Shadow Meld, Heartfinder, and so on. His SPITE unit governs physical augmentation, weapon generation (the Atticus), and tactical HUD support. The two systems interact, but they don't collapse into each other. The Spite Menagerie Upgrade, for instance, modifies what his Summoning class ability does — not by overwriting it, but by extending its functionality in ways that feel earned. That's a design distinction that separates thoughtful LitRPG from systems that are just spreadsheets with narrative gloss.

The Atticus — a modular SPITE-grown weapon capable of shifting between a katana, chain blade, and other forms — is put to immediate, specific use in the opening chapter. When Kaden nearly falls from a wall, it's the Atticus that bites into glazed brick to give him purchase. When he overshoots a rooftop, it's the Atticus extended into a chain-sword that lets him redirect his trajectory. The system isn't explained in a vacuum; it's demonstrated under physical pressure, which is exactly how good LitRPG teaches its own rules.

For readers who enjoy progression systems with genuine mechanical teeth — comparable to the interlocking ability design in *He Who Fights With Monsters* or the dual-track resource management in *Dungeon Crawler Carl* — the Spite the Dark framework has similar ambitions and, based on this opening, delivers on them.

## Character Development: Kaden Yamaguchi and Supporting Cast

Kaden is not a standard LitRPG protagonist. He's thirty-one, multilingual, trained in special operations, and possessed of below-average Intuition and Charisma scores by his own character sheet. That last detail is quietly important — it's a mechanical admission of a human limitation that most LitRPG authors would quietly ignore.

What Renfroe does well is keep Kaden's voice dry and professional without making him cold. His banter with Samantha (the AI companion) crackles with the kind of low-stakes irreverence that makes long missions readable: *"Spice later if you please." / "Fine. Spice later."* It's efficient characterization. We understand the relationship in three lines.

Vasilis — the older handler with whom Kaden shares an earpiece and a complicated personal history — is handled with more restraint. The extract gestures at genuine emotional weight: a father who concealed a mother's existence, a faith being tested, a warmth that surfaces reluctantly. Renfroe doesn't overplay it. The scene where Kaden simply chooses to leave the subject alone is more revealing than a confrontation would have been.

Paisley is the breakout character of this opening section. Her character sheet is among the most inventive creature designs in recent dark LitRPG — the mechanics of her Ziltrax Locksmith class are specific, her limitations are real, and her personality is genuinely charming. The moment she grabs a purple sticker and makes a bird-like sound of pleasure while her jaws work on it is the kind of precise, strange detail that sticks.

## World-Building: The Madness, SPITE, and the Faction Layers

The [dark LitRPG](/blog/what-is-dark-litrpg/) world Renfroe has constructed is operating on multiple simultaneous layers — Earth politics, Catalyst factions (including Gentex and the Deltas), the Madness as a parallel dimension, and the Chronarchs as a time-aware upper tier of the conflict. The opening Chronarch Missive, written as an in-world document warning against temporal intervention, does real work establishing the cosmological stakes without resorting to expository monologue.

The Washington, D.C. setting grounds everything usefully. An attic full of body bags, a killing floor lined with plastic, a senator with a bodyguard and a cyst escort — this is political thriller infrastructure being used as a delivery mechanism for LitRPG content, and it works. The procedural detail of the surveillance operation feels authentic, which matters when your protagonist is supposed to be a world-class operative.

The Madness is treated with appropriate strangeness. It's not just a monster-spawning dimension; it's a place where beings exist across geological time, where the logic of summoning and bonding operates on terms that predate human civilization. Samantha's remark that Paisley *"may not have done this precise activity before, but she's quite wise. In her own way"* is a genuinely elegant piece of alien characterization.

## Prose Quality and Pacing

Renfroe's prose is functional and precise, which is the correct register for a story told from the perspective of an assassin who thinks in tactical assessments. He doesn't overwrite action. The rooftop sequence — leaping, sliding off glazed brick, calling the Atticus, redirecting via Momentum Array, catching the window ledge — is blocked clearly enough to follow beat by beat, which is harder to achieve than it looks in this genre.

The character sheet front-matter is substantial and front-loaded, which is a genuine ask of the reader. But unlike some LitRPG series where the sheet exists purely as a reference document, here it's a narrative promise. Every ability listed in those opening pages appears in active use within the first chapter. That's craft discipline worth noting.

Pacing in the extract is tight. There's no excess scene-setting, the faction complexity is gestured at rather than explained, and the horror of the body-bag attic lands without being belabored. If the series maintains this economy through its full length, it belongs among the [best LitRPG books](/lists/best-litrpg-books/) in the dark fantasy subgenre.

## How Does Shadow in Madness Compare to Other LitRPG Series?

Readers looking for books like *Dungeon Crawler Carl* — specifically the combination of dark humor, high stakes, and mechanical creativity — will find *Shadow in Madness* operates in adjacent territory, though with a more grounded, spy-thriller sensibility and less satirical distance. The [books like Dungeon Crawler Carl](/lists/books-like-dungeon-crawler-carl/) list is a reasonable starting point for readers new to this corner of the genre.

The dual progression system and real-world setting also invite comparison to [system apocalypse](/blog/what-is-system-apocalypse/) titles, though Renfroe's tone is closer to dark espionage than post-collapse survival.

## Who Is Shadow in Madness For?

**Read this if you want:**
1. A summoner-class LitRPG where the summon is genuinely strange and well-characterized
2. Dual progression systems that interact meaningfully in scene
3. Dark fantasy LitRPG with a grounded, near-contemporary Earth setting
4. Assassin protagonists who are competent without being omnipotent
5. Political faction complexity layered over active mission structure

**Skip this if:** You prefer lighter progression fantasy with minimal gore, or you need a complete series before starting. *Spite the Dark* is ongoing.
