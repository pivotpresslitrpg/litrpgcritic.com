LitRPG authors routinely hire for unusually complicated books. An editor may be checking prose, progression, continuity, and a status system at the same time. A narrator may need to carry a sprawling cast for hundreds of thousands of words. Cover art has to signal fantasy, progression, and a specific subgenre before a reader ever reaches the blurb.

The usual way to find those people is still painfully improvised. Ask around, search old threads, open twenty portfolio tabs, and hope availability and genre experience line up.

[LitRPGTools.com](https://litrpgtools.com) has turned that problem into an actual product: a live [Industry Professionals directory](https://www.litrpgtools.com/industry-professionals/) for vetted specialists who know LitRPG and progression fantasy. Its newest expansion also opens a dedicated application path for visual artists.

## This Is More Useful Than a Flat Vendor List

The directory lets authors search professionals and filter by role: editor, narrator, cover artist, beta reader, or another professional specialty. Availability filters distinguish people accepting work now from waitlists, later bookings, and closed schedules.

The genre controls are the clever part. Authors can look for professionals comfortable with dungeon core, apocalypse, crafting, cultivation, progression, cozy, or dark LitRPG. A technically strong editor who dislikes system-heavy fiction may still be the wrong editor. The same is true of a narrator whose performance style does not suit the book’s tone.

Authors can also filter around public package pricing and sort by best match, recent approval, profile views, or name. Some professionals publish rates; others invite a conversation. Either way, the page makes the first round of research faster and more structured.

## Vetted Does Not Mean “Skip Your Contract”

Every public profile in the directory is presented as vetted. That is valuable because an open marketplace can quickly become an exercise in spam removal and identity guessing.

Vetting is still a starting signal, not a guarantee of fit. Authors should review samples, speak with references when appropriate, define the scope in writing, and confirm deadlines, revisions, payment, cancellation terms, and commercial rights. For visual work, the agreement should also record the creation process and permitted publishing and advertising uses.

The directory improves discovery. It does not outsource the author’s hiring judgment.

## Visual Artists Can Apply Now

LitRPGTools’ [Join the Community page](https://www.litrpgtools.com/join) now includes a dedicated Visual Artist path for cover design, illustration, character art, maps, typography, and related work.

That range matters. LitRPG visual production is bigger than a single ebook cover: series branding, world maps, character pieces, hardback interiors, advertising art, and typographic systems can all shape how a release is presented.

The artist roster is just beginning to form, so readers should not expect a packed cover-art marketplace on day one. The important launch fact is that the intake is public, the directory already supports cover-artist discovery, and qualified artists now have a genre-specific route to apply. Directory visibility remains subject to review.

For artists, early participation offers a focused audience: authors and publishers already working inside LitRPG, progression fantasy, and cultivation fiction. For authors, every strong applicant makes the directory more valuable.

## How Authors Should Use It

A sensible hiring pass looks like this:

1. Filter by role, current availability, and the book’s closest subgenre.
2. Compare relevant experience, profile samples, services, and disclosed pricing.
3. Build a shortlist before making contact.
4. Send a compact brief with genre, word count or art deliverables, schedule, budget, and comparable titles.
5. Put the final scope and rights in writing.

That process respects everyone’s time. It also produces better conversations than “How much for a LitRPG cover?” with no other context.

## The Verdict

This is the kind of platform feature the genre needs: not another novelty generator, but connective infrastructure for the people producing the books. The directory is already useful for finding editors and narrators, its filters acknowledge that LitRPG is not one uniform category, and the new visual-artist path gives another group of specialists a clear way in.

Authors can [search the professional directory](https://www.litrpgtools.com/industry-professionals/) today. Visual artists can review the public [application options](https://www.litrpgtools.com/join) and begin the artist path. The marketplace will earn its long-term value profile by profile—but the foundation is now visible, practical, and open for business.
