A return to Earth does not turn Ty's new abilities into an unlimited shortcut. *Theater of War*, Aaron Renfroe's second *Resonance Cycle* novel, places restrictions around advancement and protection. The result is a useful change from the opening book's preparation: now the protagonist has tools, but using them involves allocation and conditions.

The limited opportunity to protect people makes the Earth-side planning concrete. Money, recruitment, and the capabilities available outside Volar all become parts of the same strategic problem.

## Restrictions that change behaviour

Progression fantasy is often most interesting when a rule changes what the hero must do. Restrictions imposed through Ty's patron and the difference between what he can accomplish on Earth and elsewhere create that possibility. Simply staying in the safest location and grinding indefinitely is not the whole answer.

The story also continues exploring creative combinations. The attraction is a protagonist who looks for another route when the obvious resource is unavailable, rather than waiting for the narrative to erase the obstacle.

## More planning, wider consequences

The reservation is that constraints require explanation. Readers who enjoyed the first book's deliberation are more likely to welcome the planning here; readers who tolerated it only to reach a faster adventure may find the sequel still invested in preparation.

The Sanctuary material adds darker questions about what the surrounding powers consider acceptable. That gives mechanical advancement a moral context, though it further increases the information the reader must hold.

The reader fit is precise: continue for resourceful problem-solving under divine restrictions. Do not expect the return home to make the system simple, safe, or merely a convenient source of money and healing.
